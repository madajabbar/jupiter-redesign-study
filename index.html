Jupiter Swap, in plain language.
A redesign of trust, price protection, and money clarity.

Three interactions in Jupiter — the highest-volume DEX aggregator on Solana — rebuilt around one thesis: users make better decisions when the interface explains itself.

01

Price protection you can see — slippage as a spectrum, not a settings page

Problem. In Jupiter today, slippage settings live in a drawer behind a gear icon, presented as raw numbers. New traders either accept lonely defaults they don't understand or rage-set 10% and get sandwiched. The number is exposed; the concept behind the number is not.

02

Confirmation as a receipt, not a roulette wheel

Problem. The moment between signing and landing is where first-week traders churn. Today it's ambiguous: a spinner, then a hash, and no plain statement of what is about to change hands and at what guarantee. Users screenshot the spinner. That's a trust deficit, not a technical one.

Design decisions — the short write-up

One thesis, three surfaces

Every redesign here moves information the user needs to the moment they need it, and states it in the unit they think in (dollars, not percentages; verbs, not hashes). No new navigation was invented — the three changes live inside flows users already make daily.

1 · Slippage → a living price band

Slippage is a foreign word wrapped in a settings drawer. I replaced the modal-first model with an inline price-protection meter scoped in dollars ("±$0.41 max move") and paired it with a 30-second rate lock chip — the two anxieties (how bad can it get? will the price betray me mid-sign?) answered before they're asked. Advanced control remains one tap away; it's simply no longer the only path.

2 · The void → a signed receipt

The confirm screen was rewritten as a receipt: paid, received, rate-as-locked, route, gas, MEV outcome — then a closed loop back to swap. Spinner time isn't hidden; it's framed ("0.8s · confirmed at 9:41:06") so waiting reads as speed, not uncertainty.

3 · Portfolio → money view

Alphabetical token lists flatten risk. The redesign groups by behavior — core (93% of value, earning) vs watch-list (small, volatile, trending down) — and surfaces one review action per group. Percentages survive only where they inform decisions; everything else became plain language.

What I deliberately did not do

No glassmorphism, no new brand, no re-architecture. Jupiter's visual identity is strong — the upgrades are informational and typographic, so they'd ship as CSS-level changes, not a rebrand. Beautiful restraint is a feature for an app that processes billions.

Mapped to judging criteria

Solves a real problem or makes an interaction easier?
Each change removes a documented first-week drop-off point: settings blindness, confirm-stage churn, portfolio-meaning gap.
Design choices clearly explained?
Every panel pairs problem → decision → expected outcome in the write-up; unit-of-thought (dollars/verbs) is the recurring instrument.
Visually cohesive and polished?
One palette, one type system, tabular numerics everywhere money appears; before/after share the grid so the deltas read clean.
Intuitive for someone new?
New-user flows contain no jargon-first states: protection is default, receipt is plain English, portfolio ends in actionable verbs.
